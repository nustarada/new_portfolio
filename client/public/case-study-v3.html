The Challenge

Indian families juggle seven disconnected channels for one household's health — home nursing arranged over WhatsApp, doctors by phone, labs in person, emergencies through a phone number 1 in 4 people can't correctly recall. No product treated routine care and crisis care as the same journey.

The Solution

A home-first health app built around five pillars, with organised home-care nursing — the service nobody had productised — as the hero. Emergency lives one tap away on a persistent tab, routed honestly through hospitals with verified ambulance availability rather than a fleet that doesn't exist.

Weeks 1–3

Research + Architecture

Published EMS and golden-hour data, competitive audit of the Indian market, task analysis of the phone-based emergency process, and the five-pillar information architecture.

Weeks 4–10

Design + Iteration

Lo-fi structure through to 34 production screens. Heaviest iteration on the emergency flow and the home-care booking model — the two pillars carrying the product's promise.

Weeks 11–13

System + Handoff

Component library, states and edge cases, accessibility passes for panic-state use, and developer handoff across iOS and Android.

Research + Discovery

Emergency care in India fails before the call is made.

Only ~20% of accident victims reach care within the golden hour, the 108 ambulance service averages 14–31 minutes, and a public-awareness survey found 1 in 4 people would dial the wrong emergency number entirely. Meanwhile the routine end — home nursing — had no organised product at all.

Exploration across all six flows — structure, states and edge cases

The Product

Five pillars, one home — walked through screen by screen.

The dashboard

01

One home for five pillars

Home-care, doctors, diagnostics, records and emergency — each one tap from the dashboard, with a 24/7 call banner and live nurse-visit tracking above the fold.

Home · hub

02

Nursing, to your door

The pillar no competitor organised. Six clinical categories — short-term, critical, chronic, post-surgical, physio, occupational — with a visible per-visit price and one booking action, replacing an opaque WhatsApp negotiation.

Home-care · the differentiator

03

Emergency that doesn't ask questions

A panicking user shouldn't recall a number or type an address. The emergency tab is persistent; the action is single and decisive, with near-zero reading load.

Emergency · persistent tab

04

An honest ambulance flow

Hospitals listed with real ambulance availability, life-support tier and ETA — call the hospital or book the ambulance. Credible beats flashy at the worst possible moment.

Select hospital · BLS / ALS

05

Records readable in one scroll

Grouped by type — appointments, prescriptions, labs, family history — plus the government RBSK health card, built for the moment you hand your phone across a doctor's desk.

Care & discovery

Emergency, in detail

Designed for a user who cannot read carefully.

Oversized single-thumb targets in the natural reach zone, zero typing on the critical path, a two-tap guard against accidental dispatch, and meaning never carried by colour alone — panic treated as an accessibility constraint, not an edge case.

The market followed a year later

When Liffo was designed, app-based emergency healthcare barely existed in India. In January 2025 Blinkit launched a 10-minute app-dispatched ambulance service in Gurugram — validating the core bet that emergency care belongs in the same app people use for everyday health.

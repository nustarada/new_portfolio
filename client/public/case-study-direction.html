The problem

A family juggles seven channels for one household's health.

The bet

One app — home-care as the hero, emergency always a tap away.

5 pillars, 34 screens, designed for panic and for planning.

The outcome

The market followed — Blinkit launched ambulances a year later.

01

The problem

India's emergency care fails before the call is made — 1 in 4 would dial the wrong number, ambulances average 14–31 minutes, and only ~20% reach care within the golden hour. And routine home-care? Arranged over WhatsApp.

Takeaway

The system fails before the call is even made.

02

The design, walked through

Scroll — the phone follows the story.

The dashboard

Step 01

One home for five pillars

The dashboard surfaces home-care, doctors, diagnostics, records and emergency — each one tap away. A 24/7 call banner and live nurse-visit tracking sit above the fold.

Home · hub

Step 02

Nursing, to your door

The pillar no competitor organised. Six clinical categories — short-term, critical, chronic, post-surgical, physio, occupational — with a visible per-visit price and one booking action. Care arranged like a service, not a favour.

Home-care · the differentiator

Step 03

Emergency that doesn't ask questions

A panicking user shouldn't recall numbers or type addresses. The emergency tab is persistent; booking an ambulance is a single decisive action with zero reading load.

Emergency · persistent tab

Step 04

An honest ambulance flow

Hospitals are listed with real ambulance availability, life-support tier and ETA — call the hospital or book the ambulance. Credible beats flashy at the worst possible moment.

Select hospital · BLS / ALS

Step 05

Records a doctor can read in one scroll

Grouped by type — appointments, prescriptions, labs, family history — plus the government RBSK health card. Built for the moment you hand your phone across the desk.

Records · by type
1 2 3

Anatomy — home-care, the pillar nobody else organised

1

A price, up front

₹300 / visit sits on the hero card with a single "Book Today". Home nursing is normally an opaque WhatsApp negotiation — naming the price is the trust move.

2

Six real clinical categories

Short-term, critical, chronic, post-surgical, physio and occupational — the vocabulary families actually need, not a generic "book a nurse" field.

3

Care that recurs, surfaced

Popular services like palliative nursing and medication management repeat weekly — so they live on the surface, not buried in search.
